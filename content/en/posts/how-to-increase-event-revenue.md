Event revenue grows in two ways: you sell each guest more than a ticket, and you sell the tickets themselves earlier and at a higher price. In practice the methods that work best are a drinks and snacks pre-order (15 to 50% of guests take it), an extra ticket to a VIP party with the artists, prepaid merch, prices that rise by date and as tickets sell, and buyers who bring their friends for cashback. Below are the 11 most popular ways, with examples, numbers and common mistakes.

A familiar picture: the house is full, the tickets are sold, and after the event it turns out the bar queue was forty minutes long, half the T-shirts are still in the warehouse, and guests kept asking whether they could meet the artist. Each of these is money guests were ready to pay but could not.

## 1. Drinks and snacks pre-order: champagne before the show, beer without the queue

A guest who has paid in advance for a glass of champagne, a beer or snacks gets the order without queuing, and you get the money before the event. At different events, as an example, 15 to 50% of guests may pre-order champagne or a glass of wine, depending on the format of the event, and at some concerts and festivals up to 30% buy their beer in advance precisely so they do not have to queue.

**The numbers.** For an event with 1,000 guests, a 30% pre-order means 300 paid orders before the doors open. The bar knows how much to buy and how many people to put on the counter, and the queue at the interval gets shorter for everyone.

**How to set it up.** Drinks and snacks are sold through a shop next to the tickets: the buyer adds them to the same order as the ticket. You organize the hand-out at the venue, for example a separate counter for pre-orders. The shop in ArenaSoldOut.com is a separate module with a separate fee.

**A common mistake.** Selling the pre-order without a separate pick-up point for it. Then a guest with a paid beer stands in the general queue and will not pre-order next time.

## 2. An extra ticket to a VIP party or a reception with the artists

Some guests are ready to pay noticeably more for a private party after the concert, a reception or a meeting with the artists. Such a ticket often costs much more than the main one, and if the artists are well known, it can create real excitement. There are only a few places, so the scarcity takes care of itself.

The party does not have to happen somewhere else. More precisely, it often does not need another place at all. It is often held right at the event venue, in a separate area, and then no extra rent is needed.

**The numbers.** 40 places at the party for 800 guests. Even if only 5% of guests buy it, it is a separate line of revenue that does not need a bigger venue.

**How to set it up.** In ArenaSoldOut.com this is an extra category: it cannot be bought without a main ticket for the same session, and you set the quota yourself. More on categories and prices: [promo codes, price windows and steps](/product/pricing-tools).

**Common mistakes.** Selling the VIP ticket separately from the ticket to the event itself: only guests of the event should get into the party. And sometimes the mistake is announcing it after sales have started, when most guests have already bought their tickets. It is better to open it together with sales or in the first e-mail to buyers.

## 3. Prepaid merch: make exactly as many as were bought

The usual way of doing merch is risky: T-shirts and hoodies are made in advance, taken to the venue, and some of them do not sell. Prepayment turns this around: first the guests buy, then you make the exact number in the sizes that are needed.

**The numbers.** Instead of 500 T-shirts "just in case", you make 180 that are already paid for, with the exact split by size and colour. Nothing is left in the warehouse, and the money for production came from the buyers.

This matters even more for children's celebration events, but I will write about that in another article.

**How to set it up.** In the shop a product has variants (size, colour). The buyer collects the order at the event, or you send it by post or with your own courier: you choose how.

**A common mistake.** Not setting a deadline for pre-orders. Set the end date so that production is ready in time for the event.

## 4. Early sales: cheaper for those who buy now

An early bird price gives people a reason to buy right away rather than "some time later". Money from early sales reaches you weeks or months before the event, and you can put it into advertising and into organizing the event itself.

**How to set it up.** Price windows switch automatically at the date and time you set. The buyer sees until which day the price applies.

**Common mistakes.** An early period that is too long: if the low price lasts until the last week, guests have no reason to hurry. And the second common mistake: keeping quiet about the date when the price goes up. Announce it in advance on social media and in e-mails, and many will buy before the increase. Create excitement.

## 5. Price steps: the next price opens when the current one is sold

The first 100 tickets at one price, the next 200 at a higher one, the rest at full price. Steps reward those who buy early and explain the price rise honestly: the cheaper tickets have run out.

**How to set it up.** When the current step is sold, the next one opens and sales do not stop. There can be several steps. "Smart price" in ArenaSoldOut.com suggests a ready plan of steps by quantity and by date, which you can adjust to suit you.

**A common mistake.** Not warning about the increase. Each next price increase is worth announcing in advance: it is an urgent reason to buy now and one more reason to remind people about the event.

## 6. Seat categories and the seats you hold back

Each category has its own price, quota and sales window, and unsold seats move to the next category. You hold back seats for the press, guests and partners in advance and issue invitations without commission, so the best seats do not go by accident at the base price.

**How to set it up.** We draw the seating plan for free from a PDF, a photo or a table of rows. Discounts and steps work both on seated plans and on the dance floor. More: [seating plans and seats](/product/seating).

## 7. Do not open all the tickets at once

Excitement is created in different ways, and one of them is releasing tickets in parts. When only part of the venue is on sale, it sells faster, and buyers try to get tickets for the seats still free. You can open and close sales of any seats at any moment and manage the excitement.

**How to set it up.** Close some seats or categories and open them when the current ones are almost sold out. Each category has its own quotas and sales windows.

**A common mistake.** Keeping seats closed until the last day. Open the next part while interest is high, not when sales have already stopped.

## 8. "Refer a friend": buyers become promoters

Each buyer can get a personal link and share it with friends. A friend who buys through it gets a discount, and the person who brought them gets cashback to their card after the friend's event.

**Why it pays off.** You set the size of the cashback and pay it only for those who actually bought through the link. It is advertising you pay for by results, not by impressions.

**A common mistake.** Not telling people about the programme. Better mention it in the e-mail with the ticket and on the event page.

## 9. Promo codes: urgent ones and ones for your communities

A promo code without limits quickly ends up on discount aggregator sites. Limit it to an event, a session or a category, a validity period, a minimum order and a limit per buyer.

**Urgent promo codes.** These are the ones we recommend: a code that works for two or three days pushes people to buy now and speeds up sales.

**Promo codes for communities.** Make a separate code for the staff of partner companies, for a particular online community or for any other audience. People feel the code was made just for them, and you get faster sales.

**How to set it up.** Partners, bloggers and venues get their own codes, and you can see who sold how much. One page for promoters and partners: [where to sell](/product/sell).

## 10. Abandoned cart and "Notify me when sales start"

Some buyers choose seats and leave without paying. Why? People are alive and emotional: they got distracted and forgot to pay. Any incoming call or conversation can distract. Bringing buyers back, that is, working with the abandoned cart, is one of the platform's features. After 15 minutes they automatically get a reminder e-mail, and sometimes such reminders increase sales by 15-20%. In the e-mail you can offer a discount for agreeing to your newsletter, or you can offer nothing. After the sale you will have plenty of reasons to write to your buyers: to tell them about your next events, to offer special terms, to suggest they share content on social media mentioning your company, or other options.

Before sales start, build your audience: the "Notify me when sales start" button on the event page sends an e-mail the moment sales open. The list of buyers is exported to CSV for each session, and you can upload it to your mailing service.

## 11. Service fee: a separate line in the order

A service fee as a percentage can be set for each sales channel in the countries where this is allowed. The buyer sees it as a separate line, and the money goes to your payment account together with the ticket price. How it fits with the platform commission: [pricing](/pricing).
